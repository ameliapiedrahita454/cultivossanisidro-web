How a long-haul shipment works

  1. Packed for distance. Stems are cut and hydrated in the morning, graded, packed one grade and color per box, and pre-cooled before leaving the farm.
  2. Air freight with a connection. Flights to Asia usually connect through a hub in the United States, Europe or the Middle East. Your forwarder chooses the routing; we coordinate cut and packing dates with the flight.
  3. Phytosanitary certificate and inspection. Japan, South Korea and other Asian markets inspect imported plants on arrival and generally require a phytosanitary certificate from the exporting country; ours is issued by ICA (see Japan's Plant Protection Station).
  4. Rehydration on arrival. Hydrangeas drink through stems and petals: a fresh cut and a 30–45 minute full soak revive a tired head.
Requirements, duties and accepted treatments differ by country and change over time. Your importer or customs agent confirms them before the first shipment.

Why a highland farm matters for long routes

At 2,200 m, cool nights make hydrangea stems woody and heads dense. A woody stem holds water better on a long flight and stands upright under a heavy head on arrival. That is the quality insurance for a route with a connection.

Frequently asked questions

Can I import Colombian hydrangeas to Japan or South Korea?

Yes. Colombia already exports hydrangeas to Asia — in South Korea they are among the most popular Colombian flowers. San Isidro in La Ceja, Antioquia, ships to Asian importers and florists; send your country, arrival airport, grades and volumes for a quote.

How do hydrangeas travel from Colombia to Asia?

By air freight in a cold chain, usually connecting through a hub in the United States, Europe or the Middle East. Flowers are cut, hydrated, graded and pre-cooled at the farm before departure.

What documents are needed to import cut flowers into Japan or Korea?

These markets generally require a phytosanitary certificate from the exporting country and inspect flowers on arrival. For Colombian flowers the certificate is issued by ICA. Your importer confirms the current rules.
