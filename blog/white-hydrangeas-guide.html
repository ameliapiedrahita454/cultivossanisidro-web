White Hydrangeas: A Grower’s Complete Guide

the flower that made our name

San Isidro Hydrangeas Farming · La Ceja, Antioquia · August 2026

White is where hydrangeas begin. It is the most requested color in weddings and luxury events worldwide, the base for every tinted stem, and the flower San Isidro has grown in the Antioquian mountains since 1997 — we were pioneers of white hydrangea in the region. This guide covers what buyers, florists and designers should know.

Plate — Natural white hydrangea, San Isidro

Why altitude decides quality

Hydrangea quality is written in the mountain. At 2,200 meters, cool nights slow the plant down: the head fills with more florets, the white stays clean without green shadows, and the stem lignifies — a woody stem hydrates better and holds a heavy head upright through a week of event work. That is why high-altitude Colombian white hydrangea is a fixture in premium markets from Miami to Amsterdam to Tokyo.

White as a canvas: the tinted universe

Every tinted hydrangea starts white. Through absorption tinting and hand-painting — airbrush gradients, bicolors, metallics — a white crop becomes a full palette. At San Isidro that palette spans 36 tint families and 23 techniques: 255 references, each photographed true-to-color in our catalog. For brand events we regularly match specific reference colors.

Care on arrival (pass this to your customer)

  1. Cut 2–3 cm off each stem at a diagonal with a clean, sharp blade.
  2. Deep, clean water — hydrangeas drink more than almost any cut flower. Change it every two days.
  3. Cool spot, no direct sun, away from ripening fruit.
  4. A dehydrated head is not a dead head: soak the whole bloom in cool water for 30–45 minutes. Hydrangeas drink through their petals.
The name says it all: Hydrangea comes from the Greek for "water vessel". Treat it as a flower that is mostly water, and it will give you two weeks.
